# Home Demo Design

## Purpose

Move the driving game from `/` to `/drive` and replace the homepage with a real-time demoscene introduction to the game.

The homepage should not feel like a conventional game landing page. It should be a short audiovisual work that manufactures the car, the road, and the idea of speed in front of the viewer, then invites them to take control.

This direction follows the visual principles documented in [`DEMO.md`](DEMO.md), particularly:

- luminous graphic design;
- cinematic movement through depth;
- technology presented as spectacle;
- simple, legible forms beneath elaborate effects;
- transformation instead of ordinary cutting;
- geometric precision mixed with photographic imperfection; and
- an approach/reveal/transformation/escalation/resolution structure.

The central rule is:

> Do not make generic retro CGI. Make an impossible graphic idea feel like it has been photographed.

## Concept: The Line Becomes the Road

A single luminous line introduces every element of the game. It begins as an unexplained signal, reveals the car, becomes the road, creates the world, and finally resolves into the title and invitation to play.

The piece should run for approximately 30–40 seconds. Its final composition can hold briefly and then regenerate the opening signal, allowing the demo to loop without a hard ending.

## Sequence

### 1. Signal

Begin in deep black with restrained film grain and optical noise.

A white-hot point appears with a synthetic ignition tone. It moves through depth rather than simply across the screen, leaving cyan and magenta exposure trails. The movement should establish that this is a spatial, photographed phenomenon rather than a flat graphic animation.

The viewer does not yet know what is being revealed.

### 2. Machine

The luminous trail begins sweeping around an invisible object.

Successive passes reveal the player car in stages:

1. isolated points and cross-sections;
2. glowing contour lines;
3. a complete wireframe silhouette;
4. reflective or chrome-like surfaces catching impossible colored lights; and
5. the recognizable red game car.

The transition should remain continuous. The car is not cut into the scene; it is generated by the moving light.

The reflective stage matters because it lets a simple object reorganize the surrounding light into a rich image. Chrome is a means of revealing the environment, not merely an “1980s CGI” decoration.

### 3. World

The car accelerates away from the camera.

Its tire trails unfold into a perspective road network. The camera follows as roads, intersections, buildings, hangars, barriers, lights, and trees rise from those lines as simple luminous geometry.

The world can transform through silhouettes and palettes associated with several existing maps rather than presenting separate screenshots. Each environment should grow out of the previous image:

- a road line broadens into city pavement;
- city blocks stretch into industrial sheds;
- shed edges become airfield markings;
- markings sweep outward into broad country roads.

This section introduces the breadth of the game while preserving one continuous visual event.

### 4. Excess

The car enters one long, deliberate drift around the camera.

Its skid arc repeats through depth like slit-scan or multiple-exposure photography. Smoke should read as illuminated volume and optical contamination rather than realistic gray particles.

Red and blue light enters the car’s reflections before its source is visible. A police car is first implied, then revealed. The two cars cross the composition as the camera dives between them.

Scenery stretches into an abstract tunnel of road markings, windows, and light. The pursuit escalates the spectacle without losing the clarity of the two-car relationship.

This act should express the game’s core handling fantasy: **controllable excess**. The player car looks unstable and dangerous, but its line remains graceful and intentional.

### 5. Resolution

The tunnel collapses back into black space.

The player car’s final drift trails resolve into the word:

# DRIVE

Supporting copy appears beneath it:

> **Controllable excess.**  
> Cruise freely. Survive pursuit. Drive together.

Primary action:

> **TAKE A DRIVE →**

This links to `/drive`.

Secondary action:

> **REPLAY DEMO**

A restrained credit line can reinforce the demoscene framing:

> A REAL-TIME WEBGL DEMO · CODE / DESIGN / SOUND

The final composition should remain visually alive through subtle reflected light, grain, and movement rather than becoming a static web landing page.

## Visual Direction

### Palette

- deep black;
- white-hot highlights;
- electric blue and cyan;
- magenta and saturated red;
- selective spectral color;
- the game car’s recognizable red as the final material resolution.

### Materials

- chrome and polished metal;
- dark glass;
- emissive linework;
- translucent light volumes;
- polished plastic;
- simple low-poly game geometry transformed by lighting.

### Lighting

Objects should frequently read first as luminous contours. Use hard rim light, intense bright cores, broad optical bloom, and trailing exposure. Illumination should feel staged and theatrical rather than physically neutral.

### Composition

Each shot should have one dominant motif and controlled negative space. Complexity should come from movement, repetition, reflection, and accumulated exposure—not from filling the frame with unrelated detail.

### Camera

Prefer long push-ins, orbital movement, passes through geometry, and dramatic changes in perspective. Movement should be smooth, mathematical, and clearly synchronized to the score.

### Transitions

Prefer:

- morphing;
- dissolving through light;
- streaking;
- extrusion;
- passing through an object;
- reflective revelation; and
- one image generating the geometry of the next.

Avoid ordinary cuts where a continuous transformation can communicate the same idea.

### Texture

Mathematically precise geometry should be contaminated by photographic qualities:

- diffusion;
- film grain;
- bloom;
- overexposure;
- color bleed;
- afterimages; and
- slight optical softness.

## What to Avoid

Avoid treating the project as a collection of familiar retro-futurist motifs.

In particular, do not lean on:

- decorative VHS damage;
- constant scanlines;
- terminal interfaces;
- pixel fonts;
- arbitrary grids;
- neon applied equally to everything;
- rapid glitch editing; or
- a conventional hero section placed over a looping background.

A wireframe grid is useful only if it participates in the transformation—for example, if it folds into the road. Effects should serve the visual journey rather than operate as style stickers.

## Interaction and Sound

Browser autoplay restrictions mean the visual sequence must work silently, but the complete piece should include synchronized audio.

Recommended behavior:

- The visual demo begins immediately and silently.
- A small `SOUND ON` control remains available.
- The first sound-enabling interaction creates or resumes the `AudioContext`.
- Enabling sound can restart the sequence from frame zero so the viewer receives the intended synchronization.
- An optional opening action, `RUN WITH SOUND`, may be used if testing shows that an explicit start better suits the piece.
- The final `/drive` action remains available throughout or appears early enough that nobody is trapped in the sequence.

The score should be procedural or generated from compact synthesis components. It can combine:

- a restrained rhythmic pulse;
- spectral sweeps;
- transformed engine tones;
- tire-noise textures;
- low-frequency impacts; and
- a clear resolving chord or engine launch at the title reveal.

Major visual events should land on explicit musical cues:

1. signal ignition;
2. car outline completion;
3. surface/material reveal;
4. road extrusion;
5. drift initiation;
6. police-light appearance; and
7. title resolution.

## Accessibility and Input

### Reduced motion

When `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is active, show a polished static or gently illuminated version of the final composition. It should contain the title, descriptive copy, and direct `/drive` action without requiring the full camera sequence.

### Skip and navigation

Provide an accessible `SKIP TO DRIVE` action from the beginning. Keyboard focus must remain visible, and the demo canvas must not prevent normal navigation.

### Mobile

The composition should be designed for both landscape and portrait framing. Important forms must remain within a safe central region, with camera framing adapting rather than simply cropping the desktop sequence.

The demo should scale its rendering resolution and expensive effects according to device capability. Visual hierarchy is more important than maximum particle count.

## Technical Shape

Keep the demo separate from the game runtime:

```text
index.html              Homepage demo
drive/index.html        Driving game markup
src/site/home-demo.ts           Demo timeline and rendering
src/site/home.css               Demo presentation and final actions
src/games/drive/entry.ts        Drive game entry
```

The demo may reuse focused game components such as the car geometry, but it should not initialize a complete playable map. A purpose-built scene will load faster and provide tighter control over choreography, materials, and post-processing.

Likely rendering components include:

- Three.js procedural geometry;
- emissive materials and selective bloom;
- an afterimage or custom accumulation-trail pass;
- a reflective car material for the reveal;
- instanced roads, buildings, and repeated trail geometry;
- full-screen grain, diffusion, and controlled chromatic bleed;
- a deterministic time-based timeline; and
- Web Audio synthesis synchronized to that timeline.

The sequence should be deterministic enough to debug by timestamp. Each act should have named time boundaries, and development mode should support seeking directly to an act.

## Routing Migration

Moving the game affects more than the root HTML file.

### Required changes

1. Move or reproduce the current game markup at `drive/index.html`.
2. Add both the homepage and drive page to the Vite build inputs.
3. Change the “Drive with friends” link to `/drive/?multiplayer=host`.
4. Change generated invite and response URLs in `src/games/drive/multiplayer/browser-runtime.ts` to use `/drive/`.
5. Change the multiplayer leave destination to `/drive/`.
6. Change Dyno’s “Return to drive” link to `/drive/`.
7. Update route references in project documentation and browser tests.
8. Verify both `/drive` and `/drive/` behavior in the Cloudflare deployment.

### Legacy multiplayer compatibility

Existing multiplayer links target the root page. The new homepage must detect legacy game landings and forward them to `/drive/` while preserving their query string and fragment.

Legacy indicators include:

- `?multiplayer=host`;
- `?multiplayer=join`;
- `?multiplayer=response`;
- `#invite=…`; and
- `#response=…`.

This redirect should happen before the demo initializes so an invite never briefly displays the homepage sequence.

## Production Plan

### Milestone 1: Route migration

- Establish `/drive` as the canonical game route.
- Update internal links and generated multiplayer URLs.
- Add root-to-drive compatibility forwarding for old invite links.
- Verify local, production, and multiplayer flows.

### Milestone 2: Visual prototype

- Build the deterministic timeline.
- Complete the signal, car reveal, road extrusion, and title resolution.
- Add the final `/drive` action and reduced-motion composition.
- Validate desktop and mobile framing before polishing effects.

### Milestone 3: Pursuit and environment transformation

- Add map-inspired geometry and palette changes.
- Add the long drift, accumulated trails, and police reveal.
- Refine all transitions so the sequence reads as one continuous event.

### Milestone 4: Sound and finish

- Add procedural audio and synchronization cues.
- Tune bloom, trails, grain, color bleed, and reflective materials.
- Add sound, replay, skip, and accessibility controls.
- Profile startup time, GPU cost, and mobile behavior.

## Success Criteria

The homepage succeeds when:

- it reads as a designed real-time demo rather than a marketing page;
- the car, road, world, pursuit, and title emerge through continuous transformation;
- the game’s “controllable excess” fantasy is understandable before any copy appears;
- the visual language feels luminous, spatial, glamorous, and photographically imperfect;
- the invitation to play is obvious and accessible;
- existing multiplayer links continue to work; and
- entering `/drive` remains fast and independent of the demo.

The intended viewer experience is:

> I saw a signal become a machine, the machine create a road, and the road become a game. Now I want to drive it.
